# Chapter Two: The Lyre — a first proposal

A proposal to react to, not a plan. Nothing here is built. Scripture: 1 Samuel 16:14–23, with 17:15 for the ending.

## What the chapter is about

Chapter One asked: will he go after the one that is lost? Chapter Two asks something harder for a boy who has just been anointed: can he serve the king whose place he has been promised, and do him good?

There are almost no beasts in it. If the chapter is built like Chapter One with a lyre bolted on, it will feel empty. So the lyre has to be the thing the player does, the way the staff and sling were.

## The lyre as a mechanic

**What it does.** Playing lays a calm over whatever is near: a frightened animal, an angry man, a troubled king.

**How it plays (proposed).** Hold a key to draw a phrase; a line rises and falls on screen like a breath. Release at the top of each breath and the phrase lands. Three phrases make a psalm line, which is shown and spoken. It is closer to steady breathing than to a rhythm game: no note charts, no missed-beat buzzers.

**What opposes it.** The thing being calmed pushes back. A donkey shies at a noise and the breath line jumps. The king's torment comes in waves that speed the line up. Playing well under pressure is the skill.

**Why this and not a rhythm game.** A family audience, a serious tone, and a controller or keyboard with no timing calibration. Open to challenge: see the questions at the end.

## What he earns

Same rule as Chapter One: a gift is given at a place, after a trial, and changes what he can do.

| Gift | Earned | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| The Lyre | Section 1, calming the flock so the keeper can count it | Playing calms animals near him |
| The Steady Hand | Section 2, bringing the donkey through the wolves | The breath line holds steadier under a shock |
| The King's Favour | Section 4, the first playing | Doors in the fortress open to him; the journal gains the court |
| The Shield Arm | Section 5, the drill | He can raise a shield: a block, new to the fighting |
| The Night Song | Section 6 | A longer psalm line that holds its calm for a time after he stops |

Everything from Chapter One carries over, including the pouch and its stones.

## Going back

The lyre opens things in Chapter One's places. Animals that fled him then can be calmed now: the wild goats on the crag, the ewe's lost twin, a jackal bitch with pups that leaves the path when played to. That gives the Chapter One map new reasons to revisit, which is what the map is for.

## Things I am unsure of, and need you for

1. **How musical should the lyre be?** Breath-and-release as above, or a true rhythm game, or something else?
2. **How much fighting?** As proposed there are wolves on the road and a shield drill, and nothing else. Is that too little for the game you want?
3. **How is Saul's torment shown?** A growing shadow is restrained and never shows a spirit. Is that the right tone for a family audience?
4. **Jonathan.** He is not named until 1 Samuel 18. Seen at a distance in the yard, or held back entirely?
5. **The spear.** Saul throws it in 18:10–11, after Goliath. Hinting at it here is invention. Keep the hint, or leave the chapter warm?
6. **Length.** Chapter One is now about 45 to 60 minutes. The same, or shorter?

## What I would do differently from Chapter One

- Decide each section's reason and its gift before building it. Chapter One's abilities first appeared with no cause, and had to be rebuilt.
- Make every film introduce who is on screen.
- Build each section at its full length the first time.
- Have you play each section as it is finished, not all of them at the end.
